Pazeh Woodcraft

A summer spent learning from a Pazeh elder, and a project to help recreate what the Pazeh people have lost.

Background

The Pazeh

The Pazeh is one of the many unrecognized plain indigenous groups in Taiwan, experiencing serious cultural loss: the Pazeh language is one of the UN-identified endangered languages, while many of their cultural relics were lost overseas. In recent years, the Pazeh have strived to revitalize their culture through literary works, hosting events, and recreating relics.

During the summer of 2024, I learned about the story of Pazeh. Happening to know a Pazeh elder, Daway Abuk, who makes wood works in the tribe, I hoped to pitch in with the cultural revitalization process using my experience with carpentry.

This is how my trip to Pazeh began.

Field Trip

3 Days in Puli, Nantou — The Pazeh Cultural Park

Seeing the wooden animals made by the elderly, I decided to make a human figure — a Pazeh messenger that captures the Pazeh's perseverant spirit. During the three days, I followed Daway's guidance in looking for wood pieces, shaping the branches, and using many tools I'd never used before in the carpentry classroom. For the first time, I used woods with their bark kept on. Instead of cutting the wood into shapes I wanted, we looked for wood whose natural form fit my design. We found materials in nature, using leaves as hair, seeds as eyes and brows. Upon finishing my work, I drew the Pazeh totem on a slice of wood, thereby completing the largest piece of work ever made in the cultural park.
